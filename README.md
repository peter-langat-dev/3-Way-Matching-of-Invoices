# README: Three-Way Matching Process

## 1. Purpose
Three-way matching is an internal control used by the accounts payable and procurement teams to verify that goods ordered, goods received, and supplier invoices agree before payment is approved.

## 2. Why Three-Way Matching Is Important
Three-way matching helps an organisation to:

- **Prevent overpayment:** Identify invoices charging for more goods than were ordered or received.
- **Prevent fraud and duplicate payments:** Detect unsupported invoices, duplicate billing, and suspicious transactions.
- **Improve accuracy:** Ensure that quantities, unit prices, VAT, and invoice totals agree with supporting documents.
- **Control cash flow:** Confirm the amount properly payable to suppliers before releasing funds.
- **Strengthen accountability:** Provide a clear document trail for approvals, investigations, and financial reporting.
- **Resolve supplier disputes:** Use documented evidence when addressing differences in quantities, prices, or deliveries.

## 3. Documents Required
1. **Purchase Order (PO):** Establishes what was authorised for purchase, including quantities, prices, and terms.
2. **Goods Received Note (GRN):** Records what was physically received and the quantities accepted.
3. **Tax Invoice:** Records what the supplier is charging the organisation.

## 4. How to Utilise Three-Way Matching
1. Compare the PO with the GRN to confirm that the goods received correspond to the order.
2. Compare the GRN with the tax invoice to confirm that the supplier billed for the goods received.
3. Check the unit prices, quantities, VAT, and total amounts against the agreed purchase terms.
4. Investigate discrepancies, short deliveries, overbilling, and duplicate invoices.
5. Classify each invoice as **Matched**, **Exception**, or **Unmatched**, recording the reasons and supporting evidence.
6. Approve payment only when the required checks and approvals are complete and discrepancies have been resolved or formally authorised.

## 5. Using Additional Documents
- **Debit notes:** Review and apply valid adjustments or claims to the relevant transaction.
- **Credit notes:** Verify supplier credits for returns, overcharges, or other valid adjustments.
- **Bank statements:** After payment approval, match recorded bank payments to the relevant invoices and payable amounts to confirm settlement.

These additional documents support the wider accounts payable and payment reconciliation process; they do not replace the three core documents.

## 6. Expected Outcome
The process helps the organisation pay the correct supplier, for the correct goods, at the correct amount, while maintaining reliable financial records.

**Important:** A three-way match does not automatically authorise payment. Required approvals, supporting evidence, and authorised adjustments must also be considered.

***Note**: The documents in this workbook are synthetic. Use them to practise the process, identify discrepancies, document decisions, and understand how purchasing, receiving, invoicing, and payment records work together.*
